pixels → words → strokes → style

From a photo of handwriting to the hand that wrote it.

Most systems read handwriting as shapes. We recover it as motion — the order, direction and speed of every stroke — and then write anything in that hand.

See it work

01 / pixels → words

First, find the words.

Detection runs on real pages: slanted baselines, crossings-out, margin notes, bad phone lighting.

Dear Marta, I left the blue notebook on your desk — see you Tuesday morning.

Each word located and isolated before recognition begins

02 / words → strokes

Then, recover how they were written.

A finished letter is a trace — what remains after the pen has moved on. We work backwards from that trace to reconstruct the full journey: the order strokes were laid down, the direction the pen traveled, and the rhythm of the hand that held it.

Each colour = one stroke · drawn in the order and direction the pen moved

03 / strokes → style

Then write anything in that hand.

A few words of reference is enough. The model writes text the person never wrote, with their slant, spacing and pressure.
